## Bottom line

1. **No work published since June 2026 shows a fully AI-drafted text of blog length passing Pangram 4.x with its facts kept, in a way I could check.**
   - The one strong claim is StealthGPT's vendor benchmark: 88% strict bypass on Pangram 4.1 (44 of 50 texts, 300 to 1,000 words, 10 of 10 Claude Sonnet 5.5 drafts at 0% AI).
   - It is contradicted by AIHB, run on Pangram 4.1 on 2026-11-01, where StealthGPT passed 6 of 33 (about 290-word texts, my recount).
   - An outside fact audit (arXiv 2611.00359) found StealthGPT changed 13 of 56 numbers, 6 of 23 names and 14 of 15 quoted spans.
   - Commercial humanizers as a class passed Pangram 4.1 on 19 of 330 AIHB outputs (5.8%) and 3 of 60 blog posts.
2. **IdeaLens (arXiv 2610.06778, 2026-10-05) is the most important new detection paper for attempt 2.**
   - **Instruct-model prose stays flagged even with a full human plan.** When GPT-5.6 writes from a full human outline, with instructions to add nothing, Pangram 4 still flags 92.4% of the documents.
   - **Human prose passes even with an AI plan.** When people write stories from AI-generated outlines, Pangram 4 flags only 8%.
   - So Pangram 4 mainly follows who produced the words. Its accuracy on idea provenance, when ideas and words come from different sources, is 25.9%.
   - **This sharpens attempt 1's content-source finding rather than refuting it.** An AI plan is not fatal by itself. What sinks a text is an instruct model writing the surface, or the AI wording and generic detail leaking through conditioning.
   - **The study's human writers were free to add their own details.** Henry's rules forbid that, so the 8% result may not transfer.
3. **Tested on outside data, attempt 1's copy predictor works only inside a regime.**
   - On the 330 AIHB outputs, the share of words in 8-word runs copied from the input has no rank relation to Pangram's human probability (Spearman -0.02).
   - Commercial instruct-model humanizers already copy almost nothing (median 0 to 7%) and still fail 94% of the time.
   - Copying little is necessary for a base-model rewriter, but it is not a lever on its own.
4. **The closest public analogue to attempt 1's B30/B36 rewriter appeared on 2026-10-26: Smaug-Humanizer (Abacus.AI).**
   - **Recipe:**
     - Base gemma-4-12B (a pretrained model, not instruct).
     - Supervised fine-tuning on 3,676 filtered AI-to-human reverse pairs.
     - 200 steps of group relative policy optimization (GRPO) with a detector-free reward: embedding-kernel closeness to the human original minus closeness to the AI draft.
     - Hard gates on numbers, layout and length; rewrites in chunks of about 500 words.
   - **Release:** Apache 2.0, open weights. GGUF files fit a 16 GB GPU.
   - **Pangram evidence:** only 3 screenshots, version not stated.
   - **Attempt 2 use:** it is a free, held-out-compatible baseline and the one published recipe with ingredients attempt 1 did not try.
5. **New open judges exist that are closer to Pangram than MELD.**
   - **ProseLens and IdeaLens were trained on 1 million web documents carrying Pangram 3.3.2 silver labels.** That corpus, WildOutlines, is public under CC BY-NC-SA.
     - ProseLens is the prose detector; a ModernBERT-large version runs on any GPU.
     - IdeaLens scores the ideas through a role-labelled outline.
   - **They are candidates for the local judge Henry approved on 2026-11-06.** Its agreement with Pangram 4.1 has to be checked on the project's 1,534 labels before use.
6. **2026 detection work converges on four kinds of signal:**
   - the instruct-versus-base "assistant register" in token statistics and activations;
   - lexical richness and entropy;
   - sentence-transition regularity at paragraph boundaries;
   - discourse structure.
   - Content choice matters mainly to dedicated idea detectors (IdeaLens), not to Pangram. Pangram 4 is weak on math-heavy text (32% recall on AI proofs at 0.1% false-positive rate (FPR)) and lists code, Markdown and math as out of scope.
7. **Detector-guided reinforcement learning (RL) papers from 2026-06 on still test only open detectors.**
   - These are DEPO, SHADE/DArgk, EASE, WaveHumanize, AnchorWrite and PRESS.
   - The ELOQUENT 2026 shared task found that surrogate wins transfer poorly to unseen detectors.
   - It also found that evasion often comes from simplified prose.

## Implications for attempt 2

### What the evidence supports

1. **A base-model writer for every word that ends up in the post.**
   - **The instruct-writer route is closed.** IdeaLens shows Pangram 4 flags instruct-model prose 92% of the time even when the plan is fully human. AIHB shows commercial instruct-based humanizers pass 5.8% on Pangram 4.1. Together these settle that any instruct-model sentence left in the output is a liability, including Claude as "editor".
   - **Base models are still the only route with Pangram 4 evidence.** This holds for HALO, HIP and attempt 1's own B26, B28 and B30 results.
2. **The content-source finding, restated more precisely.**
   - **Pangram 4 barely tracks idea provenance (25.9%),** and human writers carry AI plans past it (TwiceTold 8%). So attempt 1's "AI facts read AI" (B4, B5, B9) is most likely leakage, not the plan itself.
   - **How the leak would work.** The base writer, conditioned on AI-worded notes, reproduces AI wording and AI-typical generic detail. That generic detail is what IdeaLens's analysis finds AI-like: unverifiable numbers, claims larger than their evidence, details that fit any subject.
   - **This points to an untested manipulation.** Give the base writer an IdeaLens-style outline, with discourse roles and short abstract content, instead of fact lists in the extractor's words. Measure Pangram against B5's fact-list arm.
   - **Caveat.** TwiceTold's writers could add their own specifics, which Henry's rule forbids. B6 also found that cutting generic facts did no better than a random cut.
3. **Sections of about 500 words or less, then join.** Smaug's card, StealthGPT's advice and attempt 1's B36 (first 300 words 11 of 32 Human against 1 of 32 whole) agree. The 512-token windows and the 90% Human rule make each window a separate test.
4. **Keep equations, code and Markdown out of the scored text.** Pangram 4.1's card lists them as out of scope, and the math paper shows its behaviour on math is unreliable. This matches B38 (the bandits post scored 0.41 with LaTeX, 0.24 without). Treat equations and code as fixed blocks the rewriter never touches.

### What the evidence undercuts

1. **"Copy less from the draft" as the main lever.**
   - **Outside the base-rewriter regime it predicts nothing.** On AIHB, 8-word copying has no relation to Pangram (Spearman -0.02), and outputs that copy almost nothing still fail 94% of the time.
   - **Keep it as a selection rule within a base-model rewriter's samples** (where B38 measured 0.70). Do not expect a copy penalty alone to close the gap.
2. **Buying or imitating a commercial humanizer.**
   - The one positive Pangram 4.1 claim (StealthGPT) fails an outside replication.
   - Commercial tools alter facts in 35 to 65% of documents, which breaks the no-new-facts rule.
3. **Detector-guided RL or decoding against open detectors** (DEPO, DArgk's SHADE, EASE, StealthRL, WaveHumanize, AnchorWrite).
   - **No Pangram transfer.** None reports Pangram results.
   - **Surrogate wins fall away officially.** DArgk's 98.5% surrogate evasion fell to 6th place in the official ranking.
   - **The gains come from damaging the prose.** Evasion comes with simplification, repetition or doubled perplexity.
4. **Agent loops with Pangram in the loop.** These are also against Henry's held-out rule. A new independent attempt (gauthamsk, 2026-11-06) again found no gradient; its only 0% came from plagiarism.
5. **Register tricks** (pre-1923 prose, stream of consciousness, from UTS). They beat open detectors, but they are wrong for a technical blog and were never tested on Pangram.

### Methods attempt 1 did not try, with some evidence behind them

1. **Run Smaug-Humanizer as an off-the-shelf baseline, at zero cost beyond Pangram credits.**
   - **Fit:** Apache 2.0, a base-model start, and the Q8_0 GGUF fits the 16 GB RTX 5080.
   - **Inputs:** the B36 unseen-writer posts in 500-word chunks, and Henry's two posts.
   - **Scoring:** the claim check and the held-out Pangram scans.
   - **Why it is cheap to learn from:** it tells attempt 2 in one batch whether a better-trained public rewriter already beats B36's 1 of 32 (AI share 0.38). It also gives a direct comparison for the recipe below.
2. **Copy Smaug's two training ideas into the B30/B36 rewriter.**
   - **(a) Filter training pairs for "real rewrites".** Drop pairs whose AI side keeps too many human sentences. Smaug says clean pairs gave most of the gain.
   - **(b) Add a short GRPO stage with a detector-free distribution reward,** k(y, human) minus k(y, AI draft) on embeddings. Use hard gates for numbers, layout, length and spelling, plus the project's claim check as a gate. Set KL 0.05 and stop early, at about 200 steps; 400 degraded quality.
   - **No detector in the loop,** so Pangram stays held out.
   - **Evidence:** only Smaug's own qualitative report, so treat it as a hypothesis.
3. **A Pangram-distilled local judge from public labels.**
   - ProseLens and ProseLens-ModernBERT-L were trained on 1M Pangram 3.3.2 labels, and WildOutlines is public (CC BY-NC-SA, non-commercial, which suits a personal research project).
   - Score attempt 1's 1,534 Pangram 4.x-labelled texts with ProseLens-ModernBERT-L before using it.
   - **The bar to clear:** within-section ranking better than "copies least" (B38's failure).
   - **Upgrade path if it tracks better than MELD:** fine-tune it further on the project's labels, under the 2026-11-06 local-judge exception with the final test set reserved.
4. **IdeaLens as a plan-level diagnostic, not a target.** Score the outlines of drafts and outputs to see whether rewrites that read Human also shift idea provenance. If Pangram flips while IdeaLens does not, the remaining gap is in the prose, not the plan. The thresholds were fitted with a Gemini 3.7 Flash extractor, so a Claude extractor needs its own calibration on human posts.
5. **Constrained RL rather than weighted rewards (DEPO's Lagrangian form),** with claim support as a hard constraint. Evidence exists only against open detectors, so use it only if item 2's reward is adopted, to keep faithfulness from being traded away.
6. **Batch homogeneity is a future risk to watch.** Test-time adaptation detectors (2606.25152) catch a pipeline by how alike its outputs are, not by any one text. If Henry publishes many posts through one rewriter, vary seeds, models and section plans.
