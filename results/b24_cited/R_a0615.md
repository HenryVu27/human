Iodine is essential in the synthesis of thyroid hormones, such as thyroxine and triiodothyronine. [2] Thyroid hormones are essential for proper metabolism, growth, and development.

Iodine deficiency is a lack of the trace element iodine, an essential dietary nutrient. [3] Iodine deficiency is the leading preventable cause of intellectual disabilities. [2]

Iodine deficiency may result in metabolic problems such as goiter. [3] Iodine deficiency is common where dietary iodine is low, typically remote inland areas where no marine foods are eaten. [3]

The thyroid gland needs iodine to produce the hormones that regulate the metabolic rate in most tissues.

Toxicity may occur when iodine is administered in very high doses. Too high iodine intake, such as from overdosed supplements, can have toxic side effects including hyperthyroidism and hyperthyroxinemia. [3]
