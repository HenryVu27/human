Body language is a type of non-verbal communication, which consists of body posture, gestures, facial expressions, and eye movements. [2] Humans send and interpret such signals almost entirely subconsciously. [2]

It is important to keep in mind that different cultures have different meanings for the same body language sign, and each culture has its own gestures, body positions, and movements. [2]

The study of nonverbal communication started in 1872 with Charles Darwin's The Expression of the Emotions in Man and Animals. [3]

The rule that states that 93% of all communication lives outside language is a common claim. The numbers come from Albert Mehrabian's studies on the relative importance of verbal and nonverbal signals in 1967. [1] These studies dealt with communication of positive or negative emotions via single spoken words, like "dear" or "terrible". [1]

Mehrabian measured how people judged feelings and attitudes when someone's words clashed with their tone or expression. He found that the disproportionate influence of tone of voice and facial expression becomes effective only when the situation is ambiguous. [1] Thus, when posture, eyes, and hands tell the same story as the words, people relax and believe the speaker.

The following numbers were obtained by combining the results from two different studies, which were inappropriately combined. [1] Mehrabian never claimed that 93% of all meaning lives outside language.

The interpretation of body language is open to subjective interpretation. [2]

Decoding is the ability to take encoded emotion and interpret its meaning accurately to what the sender intended. [3] Accurate interpretation of facial expression relies on combining multiple signs such as the movement of the eyes, eyebrows, lips, nose and cheeks to form an impression of mood and state of mind. [2]

Nonverbal communication involves a process of encoding and decoding, with messages being communicated and interpreted at the subconscious level. [3]

The wrong message can be established if body language does not match a verbal message. [3] In order to be effective, body language should be used to express what is true, not to manufacture what isn't.
