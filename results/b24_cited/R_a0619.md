In 1999, psychologists David Dunning and Justin Kruger published findings that people who performed worst on tests of logic, grammar, and humor tended to overestimate their performance the most.

Dunning and Kruger explained that the skills needed to do well in a domain are often the same skills needed to judge whether you have done well. When people lack the skills to accurately evaluate themselves, they overestimate their abilities. [2]

The explanation has several implications:

- The effect may inhibit affected people from addressing their shortcomings to improve. [2]

The most prominent alternative to the explanation is the statistical explanation, which holds that the effect is mainly a statistical artifact of regression toward the mean combined with the better-than-average effect. [2]

The meta-cognitive explanation holds that poor performers have not yet acquired the ability to distinguish good from bad performances. [2]

Dunning and Kruger’s research indicates that training in a task, such as solving a logic puzzle, increases people’s ability to accurately evaluate how good they are at it. [2]
