## The question and the design (yours to sharpen)

Question: does a base model's Pangram signal track (H1) the date of its web crawl, (H2) deliberate
synthetic data in its training mix, or (H0) neither, being a family quirk or noise?

A starting design. Improve it after the literature check; argue changes in the design doc.
- Stimuli: 20 to 30 human-written openings published before 2022-11-30 in two genres: personal essays
  (`~/personal/human/data/essays.jsonl`, 706 essays by 13 bloggers) and explanatory web articles
  (`~/personal/human/data/human.jsonl`, 900 FineWeb pages). Prompt with the opening as plain text (no chat
  template); score only the continuation. Check memorization against each document's true continuation
  (8-word runs) and exclude by a rule fixed in advance.
- Models, all base (never chat-tuned), chosen so that contrasts separate H1 from H2:
  - Within one family, before and after synthetic mid-training: OLMo-2 and Olmo 3 stage checkpoints, if
    they are published. This is the cleanest causal contrast.
  - A crawl-date series with documented data: Pythia (2020 data) as a pre-ChatGPT anchor, OLMo-1, OLMo-2,
    Olmo 3.
  - Release series in other families (Qwen2, 2.5, 3, 3.5 base; Mistral). Llama and Gemma are gated on
    Hugging Face: include them only if Henry grants access, and never through unofficial mirrors.
  - B1's Qwen3.5-9B-Base again at full sample size.
- Controls: the true human continuations (Pangram's false-positive rate on these documents), 2 or 3
  instruct models as positive controls, one decoding setting for every model, a repetition filter fixed
  in advance, the same quantization or none.
- Measures: Pangram's label and AI fraction per text; per-model rates with exact intervals; a model of
  the label on crawl date, synthetic-data exposure and family. Write down beforehand what each hypothesis
  predicts for each contrast and what result would refute it.
- Optional phase 2, only if phase 1 points to H1: measure the AI share of the crawl itself, sampling web
  documents by crawl year (Common Crawl or FineWeb dumps) and scanning them.
